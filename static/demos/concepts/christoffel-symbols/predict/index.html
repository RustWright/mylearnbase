Why this shape. Terms 1 and 2 are $\dot M\dot q$ split into two equal halves (the same sum with $i$ and $j$ swapped): equation $k$'s own row of $M$ changing as the system moves, so k stays in the row. Term 3 is $-\partial T/\partial q_k$: how the kinetic energy changes when you nudge coordinate $k$, so k moves to the bottom and $i, j$ take the row and column.

The cube: every $\partial M_{ab}/\partial q_c$

Equation : its four coefficients, then its row of $C$

Row i, column j: the coefficient on $\dot q_i\dot q_j$. Click a cell to load it.

Convention: $c_{ijk}$ with $k$ = the equation (row of $C$), as in Spong, Hutchinson and Vidyasagar. Books order the slots differently, so always check which one is the equation. With $n$ coordinates the cube is $n\times n\times n$; the recipe doesn't change.
