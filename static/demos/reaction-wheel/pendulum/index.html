Why is this so hard?

Upright is unstable: left alone, a small tilt multiplies by e (about 2.7) every 0.11 s. Time here runs at an eighth of real speed, so it is humanly possible. The real one needs a controller deciding every millisecond.

Spinning the wheel one way pushes the arm the other. But the motor can only give 0.15 N·m, and its torque fades as the wheel nears top speed (about 1,400 rpm). Hold the arm tilted and the wheel keeps speeding up until it can't push any more. To last, you have to bring the wheel back down, and the only way is to lean the arm the other way for a moment.

Does the solver matter?

A frictionless pendulum keeps its energy forever. The simulation can only approximate that, one time step at a time, and the method it steps with decides how well. Opens the free swing.

Try Euler at 10 ms from a swing near the bottom, then switch to RK4.

What the linear model predicts

Linearized about each rest position, the model gives eigenvalues that predict the motion near it. Each test runs the full nonlinear simulation (RK4, 1 ms) and compares.
